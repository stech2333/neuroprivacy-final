# Figure legends

These four figures use the formally audited reruns on the registered cohorts. Each source CSV contains the aggregate values plotted.

**Fig. 1 | Training-record exposure in prespecified EEG panels.** **a**, H, W and R are disjoint roles within each controlled allocation. H was used in training, W was held out from the same session and R was held out from a later session. **b**, Mean H−W concordance with 97.5% percentile bootstrap intervals over the original person pairs. Dreyer and OpenBMI use CBraMod after adaptation of the final three blocks, averaged over the original seeds within pairs. Stieger uses TS-LR with a block-disjoint H/W split and 21 pairs. **c**, Interaction between adaptation depth and the H−W difference, calculated within pairs. **d**, W−R estimates for OpenBMI and Stieger. The intervals show sampling uncertainty around the cross-day contrast. The two primary Dreyer tests are one-sided paired t tests with Holm-adjusted p values of 6.87 × 10⁻¹⁵ and 2.42 × 10⁻⁹. [PDF](fig1.pdf) · [SVG](fig1.svg) · [PNG](fig1.png) · [Aggregate source data](fig1.csv)

**Fig. 2 | Own-history utility under equal calibration and test budgets.** **a**, The target, frozen background representation, same-day calibration and held-out scoring records are shared across contrasts. U1 and U2 use the same local algorithm and H40 budget. **b**, Mean reduction in Brier score relative to same-day calibration alone. **c**, History and evaluation windows. **d**, Mean reduction relative to an equal quantity of matched-partner history. Stieger and OpenBMI intervals are 97.5% paired t intervals. NETBCI averages across days within each participant before inference, with a 97.5% bootstrap interval over 12 participants for **b** and a paired t interval over six original pairs for **d**. The two primary Stieger tests use the same five-test Holm family, with adjusted p values of 0.01053 and 0.01114. [PDF](fig2.pdf) · [SVG](fig2.svg) · [PNG](fig2.png) · [Aggregate source data](fig2.csv)

**Fig. 3 | Local structured curvature approximates the exact shared head.** **a**, The background representation, fitted head and curvature are frozen for local use. **b**, Stieger C8 comparison across 22 original pairs. For balanced accuracy the plot shows `(ΔBA + 0.03)/0.03`. For Brier and prediction MAE it shows `(0.005 − ΔBrier)/0.005` and `(0.01 − MAE)/0.01`. Deltas are local minus the exact shared task head. Points are means and lines are 97.5% paired t intervals. The joint test uses the maximum of the three component p values and the Holm-adjusted joint p is 1.08 × 10⁻⁷. **c**, The approximation uses the background optimum, dominant curvature directions and a residual diagonal. **d**, The upper 97.5% pair-level t bound for the mean prediction MAE and array storage for four methods on the same Stieger H40/C8/U40 panel. The horizontal line indicates the protocol MAE limit. [PDF](fig3.pdf) · [SVG](fig3.svg) · [PNG](fig3.png) · [Aggregate source data](fig3.csv)

**Fig. 4 | Offline shared-artifact invariance and the depth-adaptation boundary.** **a**, Learning locations in the OpenBMI comparison. M0 fits a task head with a frozen pretrained representation. M1 adapts the shared encoder with target history. M2 freezes a background-adapted encoder and fits a shared head with the target history. M3 updates the head locally. **b**, Open markers show the maximum absolute change in fixed shared probe predictions and bars show the mean absolute change in local predictions after a history swap, based on the reproduced controlled offline runs. **c**, Checks exercised in the offline reference paths. **d**, Brier differences between M3 and M1 on 18 OpenBMI pairs. Lines are 97.5% paired t intervals and the dashed line indicates the +0.005 Brier margin. Deep-adaptation acceptance also requires the balanced accuracy margin. The reference paths cover the tested local devices and simulated synchronization. [PDF](fig4.pdf) · [SVG](fig4.svg) · [PNG](fig4.png) · [Aggregate source data](fig4.csv)
